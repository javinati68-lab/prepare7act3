Step 2: Paragraph Structuring

Essays for this exam are always written for a teacher, meaning they require a neutral or formal register and clear organization. Construct your essay using this framework:

Cambridge B2 First (FCE): Essay Framework

Write 140–190 words for the whole essay, not for each paragraph. Address both given notes and a distinct idea of your own. Give a clear opinion supported by reasons. Use a neutral or formal tone.

Suggested five-paragraph plan

This is a practical structure, not a compulsory exam format. The word ranges below are planning guides.

  • 1. Introduction (about 25–30 words)
    Paraphrase the question and briefly state your position.
    Starter: "Reality TV and talent shows can influence young people’s ambitions. In my view, …"
  • 2. Given point 1: Why these shows are popular (about 30–40 words)
    Make a point, explain it, and give a brief example. Possible angles: entertainment, competition or the appeal of success stories.
    Starter: "One reason these programmes attract viewers is … This is because …"
  • 3. Given point 2: Effects on young people (about 30–40 words)
    Explain a possible effect and connect it to your opinion. Consider inspiration as well as unrealistic expectations.
    Starter: "However, these shows may … For example, …"
  • 4. Your own idea: Realistic routes to success (about 30–40 words)
    Add a separate angle, such as the importance of education, practice and achievable goals. Explain why it matters rather than repeating point 2.
    Starter: "Another important consideration is … Therefore, …"
  • 5. Conclusion (about 25–30 words)
    Bring the argument together and answer "Do you agree?" clearly. Do not introduce a new argument.
    Starter: "On balance, I … because …"

Step 3: Self-Evaluation Check

Review your completed draft against these core exam requirements:

  • Content: Does the paragraph focus strictly on the prompt without including irrelevant information?
  • Communicative Achievement: Is the tone appropriate and effective for the target reader?
  • Organisation: Do the sentences flow logically using linking words and cohesive devices?
  • Language: Does the text demonstrate a good degree of control over grammatical forms and use everyday vocabulary appropriately?

Write your essay below.

Reality TV and talent shows have become extremely popular. While some argue they offer harmless entertainment, they can heavily influence young people's ambitions. In my view, these programmes promote unrealistic goals.

One reason these shows attract viewers is the appeal of instant fame. This is because audiences enjoy watching ordinary people achieve spectacular success overnight. For instance, singing competitions often turn unknown individuals into celebrities.

However, these shows may negatively affect young viewers. For example, many teenagers develop unrealistic ideas about their future, believing they can become rich without putting in effort. Furthermore, some stop caring about their schoolwork, assuming education is unnecessary for success.

Another important consideration is that real success requires dedication and hard work. Therefore, young people should focus on building tangible skills and pursuing achievable career paths rather than relying on the slim chance of television stardom.

On balance, I agree that young people need more realistic ambitions. While talent shows are entertaining, relying on them as a career model is detrimental to a secure future.
